Bringing the first dyed fabric back into stock

One greige lot goes to the dyer and comes back as finished fabric, booked into stock with one action on the job — Receive from processor — then made ready for the cutting table. Seven phases; each ends with what you should see on screen. The first has happened: on 19 September the first part of DJ-ESSKY085LS-002 — 852.1 m at 54", back from Aryan Dyeing — became the first dyed fabric ever booked into this system's stock. Its second delivery is what phase 4 now covers.

Before you start

  • The dyer or printer exists as a supplier.
  • The greige you will send is already in greige stock — its purchase order was received. Twelve lots are in stock today.
  • You know which warehouse the dyed fabric will be received into.
  • Pick a small lot for this first run. The point is to see the whole loop work, not to move a season's cloth.
01

Raise the job work order

Manufacturing → Job Work Dashboard → Job Work Orders → New Job Work Order

  1. Process Type * — Dyeing (or Printing).
  2. Material * — Fabric (cloth).
  3. Style — leave it blank for this run. With a style filled in, the dialog sends you to the Processing page instead. A blank style makes this a stock job, which is the simplest path and the one this runbook follows.
  4. Processor * — the dyer.
  5. Quantity * — metres of greige you are sending.
  6. Rate * — the agreed rate. Dyers are paid per metre returned, not sent.
  7. Colour, Finished Width (inches), Expected Shrinkage (%) — all three appear because there is no style. Colour is required. Shrinkage must be under 100.
  8. Expected Return — the date you need it back. Remarks — optional.
  9. Click Create Draft JWO.
You should see
  • The order in the list with status Draft and a job number like DJ-…
02

Send the greige out

Open the new order from Job Work Orders

  1. Click Approve. Status becomes Approved.
  2. Click Issue to Processor. This button exists only while the order is Approved.
  3. Under Greige Lots *, pick the lot and the quantity. The total must read “matches the order”. Every row must be the same greige — one order sends one cloth.
  4. Vehicle Number if you know the truck. Do not type a challan number — the system assigns it.
  5. Click Issue & Create Challan.
You should see
  • A confirmation with the outward challan number — write it on the physical challan that travels with the cloth.
  • The job at Issued; on Manufacturing → Dyeing & Printing it shows as At Mill.
  • The greige lot's available metres reduced by what you sent.
03

At the mill — nothing to do in the system

Until the cloth comes back

When the dyed fabric arrives at the gate, note these on paper before anyone touches the screen. They are exactly what the receive dialog asks for, in one go.

Write down at the gate

  • The processor's challan number on the paperwork that came with it.
  • Number of thans (folded bundles) and either the total metres or the fold length in centimetres. Fold length is centimetres, and the system refuses anything of 1000 or more — a metre figure typed here will be rejected.
  • The measured width in inches. This width is stamped on the finished fabric's name.
  • The date it actually arrived.
  • The quality — A (good), B (minor defects) or Reject — and the defect metres, if any. Quality is recorded in the same dialog as the receipt now; there is no separate approval step.
04

Receive from the processor — one dialog, one click

Open the job → Actions card → Receive from processor

There is no GRN form to fill and no separate approval any more. The receipt is filed for you, already accepted, and the stock is booked in the same moment.

  1. Open the job (Manufacturing → Job Work Dashboard → Job Work Orders → the job) and click Receive from processor in the Actions card. The dialog is titled Receive from and the processor's name. The green Receive from processor icon on a row showing At Mill — Manufacturing → Dyeing & Printing, Job Work Orders tab, or the Dyeing / Printing page — opens the same dialog.
  2. Read Expected back — the greige sent minus the expected shrinkage. Maximum you can receive appears once you start typing a quantity.
  3. Entry mode — Total metres is simplest: fill How much came back (MTR) *. Than count and Fold length (cm, under 1000) can be recorded alongside it; leave the metres blank and the quantity is worked out as than count × fold length instead. Than-wise: click Add than for every than and type its metres. Bale-wise: Add bale, then Add than inside each bale. The Detail sum shows the running total; every row needs metres above zero.
  4. Measured width (inches) — from your gate note. It is stamped onto the finished fabric.
  5. Their challan no. — the processor's challan number.
  6. Into warehouse * — only real stores are listed; with a single store it is already filled in. Date received * — the day the cloth arrived (defaults to today; it cannot be before the day the greige was sent).
  7. Quality (optional) — A - Good, B - Minor Defects or Reject — and Defect metres if any. This is the only place the quality of a job-work receipt is recorded.
  8. This is the final delivery — the tick-box ticks itself once what you are receiving, together with any earlier parts, reaches the expected quantity (less the processor's tolerance). Untick it if more is still to come: this part is booked into stock and the job stays open as Partial Receipt. Tick it yourself to close the job short.
  9. If the quantity is short beyond the job's tolerance and the final-delivery box is ticked, a warning names the metres beyond the allowance and says a debit note against the processor will be needed before the job can close. You can still go ahead.
  10. Click Receive & add to stock — it reads Receive part & add to stock while the box is unticked. The button stays greyed out until you have a quantity, a warehouse and a valid date.
You should see
  • Final delivery: the job at Stock Updated, showing received metres, actual shrinkage %, than count, fold length, width and quality.
  • A part: the job at Partial Receipt, its Quantities card reading Received so far … of … expected — … still to come. The part is in stock already.
  • In the job's Actions card: Return receipt GRN-… (or Return receipts (2) after a second part) and Print Inward Challan.
  • If the loss was larger than the expected shrinkage, an Abnormal Loss Detected banner on the job. That is information, not an error — but a debit note is needed before the job can close.

The second delivery — DJ-ESSKY085LS-002 today

  • Open the job (it shows Partial Receipt) → Receive from processor. The dialog now shows Received so far (852.1 m) and Maximum you can still receive.
  • Enter the second challan's metres, its own Their challan no. and Date received, the measured width, and the quality.
  • Once the total reaches what is expected, This is the final delivery ticks itself — leave it ticked and click Receive & add to stock. If the dyer is still short after this delivery and nothing more is coming, tick it yourself: the job closes short and the shortfall is treated as loss.
  • Each part keeps its own stock lot and inward challan; shrinkage and the loss split are worked out once, on the total of all parts.
If the screen is slow, do not click again — open the job and check its status first. A job whose final delivery is already recorded reads Stock Updated and a repeat is refused with “has already been received”.

05

Check what that one click booked

Nothing to approve — this phase is only looking

  1. Procurement → GRN (Goods Receipt): the receipt is already there at Accepted, badged Job work return, in your name — one receipt per delivery. There is no Pending QC and no Approve button for it. Opening it shows the job it belongs to, not a purchase order.
  2. The fabric stock list: a new lot in the warehouse you chose, dated with your Date received, named from the greige, colour and width — like Cotton Poplin - Solid/Dyed - Navy - 54" — with a code like FAB-STK-0001 and the quality grade you gave. A job received in parts has one lot per part, under the same fabric.
  3. Back on the job: Print Inward Challan — the challan from the processor was raised for you; you do not create it. The metres, width and shrinkage on the job should match your gate notes; with parts, the shrinkage is on the total.
Done when
  • Receipt Accepted, job Stock Updated, and the dyed fabric in stock with the right metres and width.
Counted wrong? Ask an admin to reverse that one receipt: its lot and inward challan go, the other parts stay, and the job's total is recomputed. Reversing the final part re-opens the job as Partial Receipt, so the last delivery can be entered again. A reversal is refused once any of that lot has been used or reserved, so check the numbers the same day.
06

Close the order

Back on the job work order, once the processor's invoice is in

  1. Click Close Order and enter Processor Invoice Number *. Closing is refused while an abnormal loss has no debit note against the processor — raise the debit note first if the banner appeared. Dyers are paid per metre returned: the bill should match the received metres, not the greige sent.
  2. Tell the office it is done, and what was confusing. This is the first time this loop has run for real; anything awkward is worth reporting.
Done when
  • The job is Closed, its return receipt is Accepted, and the dyed fabric shows in stock with the right metres and width.
07

Before it can be cut

Stock alone is not enough — cutting checks samples, CAD and the fabric link

Before a run can go to cutting the system checks three things: an approved Size Set Sample on the style (which needs an approved PP Sample before it, which needs an approved FIT Sample), an approved Production CAD with an average (a pending or rejected one does not count), and a way to find the fabric — through the style's fabric slot, or through a Production CAD row created against the actual lot. A job raised with a style fills the fabric link on its own, provided the style has one fabric on that greige and finish (since 23 Sep 2026 this works even when the order's BOM line was not tied to a CAD row). A job raised without one — the stock job in phase 1 — does not, and the lot will not even be offered in CAD Planning until you link it by hand. Every step below was run end-to-end against the real system on 16 Sep 2026 and again on 23 Sep 2026 (cutting-first-run.test.ts).

  1. Approve the three samples — FIT, then PP, then Size Set. Manufacturing → Sample Tracking → New Sample. Customer * is the house brand (a sample always needs a customer, even for stock production), Sample Type * FIT Sample, Style this style, Required By * any date → Create Sample. Then walk it through the list's Quick Action column: Start Progress → Mark Complete → Mark Sent → Record Feedback → Status Approved → Save Feedback. Do this even for a house-brand style with no real buyer — the system will not accept an approval before the sample is marked Sent. Repeat for PP Sample, then Size Set Sample. The order is enforced: a PP cannot be raised until the FIT is approved, a Size Set not until the PP is. If the style already has an approved Size Set Sample, skip this step. An admin can skip a sample with a written reason — it is logged under Admin → Override History.
  2. Link the fabric to the style — only if the job had no style. Materials & Masters → Fabric Master → click the new fabric (it opens for editing) → under Allocated Styles click + Allocate to Style → choose the style and its component. If the style is already listed there, skip this step. If that component has more than one empty fabric slot, choose the one whose greige name matches. Otherwise a new slot is created, the existing CAD rows keep pointing at the old one, and the chart shows a fabric with no average.
  3. Give each received lot a Production CAD. Pre-Production → CAD Planning → find the style → Open CAD. The green Fabric Stock Available box lists every lot with its GRN number, width and metres (for example GRN2609-0080 · 52" • 852.1m) and a badge such as 2 need CAD. Click Create CAD next to each lot — one Production CAD per lot. The new Production row arrives filled in from the approved Raw Mat marker: size breakdown, pieces, layer length and CAD Average, at the lot's width. If the lot is a different width from the marker, a warning says so and the layer length and average are left empty — enter the layer length for the new width. If it answers "Cannot tell which fabric of … this lot belongs to", the lot does not match exactly one of the style's fabrics — nothing was created; check the style's fabrics and try again.
  4. Check and approve each Production row. Check the width, pieces and CAD Average, then open the row's three-dot menu → Approve. Cutting needs an approved Production CAD: a pending one, or one someone rejected (red Rejected badge — hover it for who, when and why), does not count. For a rejected one, click Create CAD on its lot again.
  5. Send the production run to cutting. Manufacturing → Production Runs → open the run → Push to Cutting. Then in Fabric Issuance on the same page, tick the lot and click Issue to Cutting. If the page answers "Cannot push to cutting: No Size Set Sample exists for this style", go back to step 1 — the run and the fabric are fine. If it offers an Admin Override instead, do not use it to skip the samples: it bypasses every check at once and only records a reason if you type one.
  6. Manufacturing → Cutting → New Batch → pick the Production Run. Under Lot Details your lot appears under its fabric with its width. Fill Actual Fabric Width, Layers per Lay and Number of Lays — all three are required — and Create Batch is enabled.
You should see
  • In Sample Tracking, the style has a FIT, a PP and a Size Set sample, all Approved.
  • In CAD Planning, the style's Progress column shows Production done.
  • In the style's Fabric Stock Available box, every lot is green with a CAD tick, and its Production row is approved (no Create CAD button left).
  • On the Cutting Chart, Max Cuttable is a real number, not blank, and names your fabric. Once fabric is issued, the chart plans against the issued metres, not the store's.
Create Batch greyed out? A fabric on this style has no approved Production CAD with an average — the chart says "No approved Production CAD for: …". A pending or rejected Production CAD does not count. Approve it in CAD Planning (row menu → Approve).

If the system refuses

The exact messages, and what they mean

It saysWhy, and what to do
Receive from processor is not on the jobThe job has not gone out yet (Draft or Approved — use Issue to Processor, phase 2), it was already received, it is cancelled or closed, or it is linked to a purchase order (receive that one on the GRN form against its PO).
Fabric job work is received through a GRN …An old screen or link posted the job to Receive Material, which books no stock. The job is untouched — use Receive from processor on the job (phase 4).
Received quantity must be greater than 0No metres were entered, or every than row is empty. Nothing was written.
Fold length is in cm and must be under 1000You typed metres or millimetres. Fold length is centimetres.
Received … MTR on top of the … MTR already received exceeds the expected fabric … plus … over-receipt tolerance (max …)All parts together would be more than sent-minus-shrinkage allows. The message shows the maximum for the job as a whole. Re-count the thans of this delivery.
Date received … is before the day the greige was sent (…)The date is earlier than the send day. Use the day the cloth actually arrived.
… has already been receivedThe job's final delivery is already recorded. Open the job: it should read Stock Updated with its Return receipt(s). If a genuine further delivery arrived after that, an admin reverses the final receipt (which re-opens the job as Partial Receipt) and it is entered again.
… link the job to its greige lot or requirement, or set its finished fabricThe job was not issued from a greige lot, so the system cannot tell what fabric it is. Ask the office to link the job to its greige lot, then receive again.
… is cancelled — its stock was already credited backCancelled jobs cannot be received; their greige went back to stock. If the mill really returned material, the office must re-open the job first.
The warehouse box reads Could not load — open to retryThe server was busy for a moment. Open the box again — the list is fetched afresh.
The Production CAD for style … is waiting for approvalPush to Cutting needs an approved Production CAD. CAD Planning → the Production row → three-dot menu → Approve.
The Production CAD for style … was rejectedSomeone rejected it (hover its red Rejected badge to see why). Click Create CAD on the lot again, check the new row and approve it.
No Production CAD for style …No lot has a Production CAD yet. CAD Planning → Create CAD on each lot in Fabric Stock Available, then approve.
This Production CAD has no average yetApprove was refused: the row has no layer length or size breakdown (usually because the lot's width differs from the marker). Fill Layer (M) and the Size Breakdown, save, then approve.
This lot … already has a Production CADEach lot gets one. Find it in the Production section of the table.
An unexpected error occurredNot one of the above. Note the time and what you clicked, and report it — this is exactly what the first run is for.
